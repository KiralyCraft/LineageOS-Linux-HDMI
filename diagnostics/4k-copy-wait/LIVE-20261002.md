## Allocation and accelerated-path validation

All automated Konsole and Firefox phases produced zero new GBM allocation failures and zero TearFree flip failures. The isolated Firefox verified hardware WebRender and FD740 WebGL. Its log recorded 66 GPU-bridge initializations, no CPU-readback fallback, and no GPU-slot import failure. The Maps resize phase completed without the prior isolated-browser IPDL failure. These observations validate the corrected allocation path during this run; they do not establish long-term crash freedom or resolve all latency.

## Xorg copy and wait measurements

An ordinary Qt Widgets Konsole window was resized or moved on the right side of the desktop. Only complete diagnostic reporting intervals entirely inside each phase were selected. The desktop and other user applications remained active, so this is a live workload measurement, not a background-free microbenchmark.

| Workload | Selected duration | TearFree copies | Mean copy submission | Mean blocking finish | Per-window p95 finish upper bounds | Time blocked in finish |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Resize, 16.7 requests/s target | 15.02 s | 831 | 0.154 ms | 5.03 ms | 8.0–8.75 ms | 27.8% |
| Resize, 60 requests/s target | 15.02 s | 897 | 0.105 ms | 3.75 ms | 5.25–5.5 ms | 22.4% |
| Move, 60 requests/s target | 10.02 s | 593 | 0.182 ms | 5.46 ms | 9.0–9.5 ms | 32.3% |

The main event loop is blocked during finish. Its duration includes completion of earlier GPU work as well as the damage copy; this is not a GPU timer isolating the last blit. Mean successful flip-queue calls were only 0.13–0.22 ms. Queue-to-callback waiting is separate and does not imply that the CPU event loop is blocked. Copy-total includes finish and must not be added to it.

At the 60-request/s target, actual requests averaged about 54.9/s for resizing and 55.5/s for movement because synchronous request turnaround occasionally exceeded the target period. XSync p95 was about 28.0 ms and 26.1 ms respectively. XSync acknowledges requests, not application repaint or physical scanout. The 16.7-target resize used 8.44 Xorg CPU seconds over 18.16 seconds; this does not demonstrate a compiler-optimization improvement over the earlier short test, which was not an equivalent controlled A/B run.

Separate full glamor reporting intervals during movement recorded 834 root-overlap temporary-copy attempts in about 15 seconds, covering 1.094 billion source-region pixels. This confirms that the temporary-buffer path is exercised heavily by ordinary window movement. Counts describe attempted operations and regions, not exact bus traffic. Resize also triggered temporary operations, but their measured regions were much smaller. No upload/readback fallback was recorded by these particular glamor-copy counters; those counters do not cover every Xorg transfer path.

Maps dragging still had 60 compositor intervals longer than 50 ms. Its DOM animation-frame median was 66.36 ms. Thus eliminating the allocation fallback did not eliminate interactive stalls. The browser's resize API acknowledgement includes browser automation and window management and must not be presented as a GPU-copy timer.

Sparse compositor updates during typing follow input cadence and must not be interpreted as a continuously animated FPS test. This run did not directly time the Mesa bridge's synchronous blit.

## Repeated MSC and stale timestamps

The original movement phase received 593 successive TearFree callbacks with repeated MSC across 10.02 seconds, while callbacks still arrived about 59 times/s. Reported UST became as much as 21.74 seconds old. A read-only `DRM_CAP_TIMESTAMP_MONOTONIC` query returned 1, matching the diagnostic clock. These callback counts are not proof of unique visible frames.

A second movement sequence compared no extra queries, periodic `drmCrtcGetSequence` calls (every sixth requested move, approximately 10/s), and no extra queries again:

| Phase | Selected callbacks | Repeated MSC | Mean reported-UST age | Mean blocking finish |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Before queries | 588 | 588 | 9654.81 ms | 5.44 ms |
| During queries | 880 | 471 | 41.37 ms | 5.77 ms |
| After queries | 880 | 601 | 476.82 ms | 5.57 ms |

All 180 sequence queries succeeded. They changed the counter behaviour but did not improve the measured finish wait or establish an interaction-speed gain. Background damage differed between phases; the final phase did not reproduce the completely stale first phase. This is evidence consistent with a vblank-reference/counting problem, not proof of a specific missing driver reference or a proposed polling workaround.

The live `/sys/module/drm/parameters/vblankoffdelay` value was `-1` and was left unchanged. The [pinned kernel's vblank implementation](https://github.com/LineageOS/android_kernel_sony_sm8550/blob/d00ba216ccda5d4fcc0d864729ae69d5b63d860c/drivers/gpu/drm/drm_vblank.c) defines negative delay as immediate disable. Its sequence query can temporarily acquire a vblank reference, while sending a flip event reports the most recently processed count/time. This explains why queries are not observationally neutral with respect to vblank accounting. The exact vendor-driver reference lifecycle still needs review.

## Engineering implications

The synchronous Xorg completion wait is now a measured cost, and ordinary movement exercises the extra temporary-copy path. A fence-driven event-loop implementation is justified as the next candidate, retaining the copy-to-scanout ordering and lifecycle rules in the main investigation README. It will not remove copy bandwidth or automatically fix browser stalls.

Investigate the vblank reference lifecycle separately before adjusting Mesa's target-MSC lead or treating completion timestamps as reliable physical cadence. The current evidence does not justify removing TearFree, substituting flush for finish, enabling an unvalidated tiled root, or changing the global vblank setting.
